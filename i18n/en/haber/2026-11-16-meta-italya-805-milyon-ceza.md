Italy's data protection authority, the Garante, fined Meta €805 million (about $887 million) on Saturday, 14 November. It is the largest fine the authority has ever issued and equals roughly 0.47 percent of Meta's global turnover.

## What happened?

The investigation focused on the "get suggestions from your gallery while browsing Facebook" feature in the Facebook app on Android. To suggest posts, the feature accessed photos and videos on the phone along with their metadata. According to the authority, the feature was on by default and worked without asking the user for specific consent or giving enough information.

The review found that Facebook requested gallery access through a single permission, and that this permission covered several functions serving different purposes. The Garante says this violates the Italian implementation of the EU's ePrivacy directive, because the law requires "free and informed consent". Italy has about 20 million Android users.

## What made the fine heavier?

The authority cited Meta's failure to cooperate with the investigation and a previous fine from 2019 as aggravating factors. The fine was issued to Meta Platforms Ireland Limited; the company had challenged Italy's jurisdiction. Meta was given 60 days to switch off the feature and obtain separate consent. The investigation did not examine whether the content was used to train AI; Meta denies this anyway. Meta did not comment at first and has the right to take the decision to court.

## Why does it matter?

A phone's gallery is most people's most private archive. The decision shows that the "you agreed once" approach is not enough in Europe, and that each purpose requires separate, explicit consent.
