On November 27, OpenAI filed a countersuit against Apple in federal court in California. The company accuses Apple of stealing its trade secrets and of discouraging hardware suppliers from working on OpenAI's future devices. The suit is a response to the case Apple filed against OpenAI in July, accusing it of poaching employees to gain access to its technology.

## What does OpenAI claim?

According to the court filing, Apple gained access to confidential information through an employee who returned to Apple after working at OpenAI for six months. OpenAI says this person had access to a new device and to the company's future hardware roadmap, and argues that, judging by the timing, taking the documents may have been the real reason they joined OpenAI.

The filing also claims that Apple encourages its employees to keep work files on personal devices and accounts, and then "traps" those who leave when they keep accessing those accounts. According to OpenAI, 400 employees have moved from Apple to OpenAI so far, and Apple wants to scare the others. As reported by the Financial Times, OpenAI also accuses Apple of using its size and wealth to stifle competition.

Apple has not yet responded to the new allegations. These claims have not been proven in court.

## The hardware race heats up

At the heart of the tension is OpenAI's push into hardware. In May 2025 the company bought io, the hardware startup founded by Jony Ive, for 6.4 billion dollars. Apple's lawsuit also targets Tang Tan, a former iPhone and Apple Watch design executive who now works in OpenAI's hardware unit. OpenAI, for its part, writes in its filing that the AI devices it is developing could beat Apple "on its own turf". ## Why does it matter?

The two partners that once brought ChatGPT to the iPhone are now rivals in the personal device market. The case shows that the AI race has moved from software to hardware and the supply chain, and that the fight for talented engineers and manufacturing capacity has reached the courts.
