The tech sell-off that has gripped US stock markets for weeks intensified on Friday, November 20. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite closed the day down 5.28 percent at 22,617.73 points, erasing all of its gains for 2026. The S&P 500 fell 3.7 percent and the Dow Jones lost 949.73 points.

## What happened?

According to UPI, the Dow and the S&P 500 officially entered correction territory after falling more than 10 percent below their peaks. As reported by CNN, the S&P 500 also closed lower for a seventh straight day, its longest losing streak since February 2020. The Nasdaq has lost about 8.7 trillion dollars in market value since its record on October 6.

Chipmakers were at the center of the sell-off. Nvidia fell 14 percent and ended the week down 22.9 percent, its worst week since October 2008. CNN cites a Reuters report that the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) is examining Nvidia's accounting practices. According to UPI, AMD lost more than 13 percent and Broadcom more than 14 percent. Anthropic, whose shares had opened 17 percent higher in a record debut the day before, also fell 8.5 percent.

## Why are investors running?

Analysts say companies are spending hundreds of billions of dollars on AI infrastructure and investors now want to see a return on that spending. Fears that data center investment could slow and the uncertainty created by Jensen Huang's departure from Nvidia also played a part. The VIX index, which measures fear in the market, rose 86 percent over the week. Investors turned to US Treasury bonds; according to UPI, Bitcoin also fell below 60,000 dollars.

## Why does it matter?

Much of the money behind the AI race comes from the stock market. As shares fall, it may become harder to raise money for new data centers, chips and models. Some strategists, however, point out that the sell-off is overdone and that valuations have dropped to levels not seen in years.
