At a press conference at the White House on November 20, US President Donald Trump said he would not allow US companies to build data centers abroad. Trump added that companies investing abroad anyway would face "tremendous" taxes and penalties.

## What did he say?

According to NBC News, Trump said he wants to stop AI infrastructure from leaving the country, arguing that a similar loss happened with chip manufacturing in the past and that it harmed the country. According to Axios, Trump claimed that if data centers go abroad, China will "take" them. Trump also openly said he had not yet discussed the plan even with his own team.

In the same remarks, Trump also addressed communities opposing data centers at home, suggesting they negotiate with the companies and get money from them.

## Can it be enforced?

The uncertainty is large. Axios points out that under the US Constitution, the power to levy federal taxes lies with Congress; it is unclear whether Trump would seek congressional approval or use existing presidential powers. Axios technology editor Ina Fried also notes that even companies building facilities in the US need data centers abroad to reduce latency, stay resilient to outages and comply with laws in some countries that require data to be processed locally.

The announcement comes at a time when data centers have become an election issue in the US. According to Ballotpedia data, in 52 of 53 local votes in 13 states in 2026, voters chose to limit data center construction.

## Why does it matter?

Data centers are the most expensive item in the AI race, and big companies need to spread these facilities across different parts of the world. If such a ban or tax takes effect, overseas investment plans will be shaken; even if it does not, investors now have a new line in their uncertainty calculations.
