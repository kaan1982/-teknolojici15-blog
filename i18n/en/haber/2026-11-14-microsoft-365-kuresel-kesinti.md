Microsoft's most widely used business services went down worldwide on Thursday, 12 November. The problem began around 12:30 p.m. US time (around 8:30 p.m. in Istanbul) and spread to at least 74 countries. According to Microsoft's latest updates, services are gradually returning after about 24 hours, but some users are still seeing delayed email and intermittent access problems.

## What was affected?

Outlook and Exchange Online were hit hardest: emails did not arrive or were delayed for hours. In some organisations, the calendar, meeting scheduling and starting new chats did not work in Teams. Services such as SharePoint, OneDrive, Copilot, Defender, Intune and Power BI were also on the list. On Downdetector, Outlook complaints peaked at between 131,000 and 140,000. In the UK, the NHS activated its contingency plans; Norwegian ministries, government systems in Japan, the US House of Representatives and courts in Singapore were also among those affected.

## What caused it?

According to Microsoft, a process responsible for establishing secure connections on the affected servers became overloaded, and the servers stopped responding. The company said it had rolled back a recent network configuration change that coincided with the start of the failure, and that the problem stemmed from an earlier network capacity upgrade. Microsoft stressed that it had found no evidence of malicious activity and that the incident had nothing to do with a cyberattack or with AI agents. The retention period for delayed emails was extended to 48 hours so that they would not be lost. A detailed incident report has not yet been published.

## Why it matters

The email, calendars and correspondence of millions of organisations depend on a single provider's cloud. The fact that one configuration change could slow down work for a whole day in dozens of countries, from hospitals to courts, has shown once again how big a risk cloud dependence is.
