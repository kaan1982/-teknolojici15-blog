AI-powered search company Perplexity announced on November 16 that it has set up a new research arm called Perplexity Frontier Research. The unit's goal is ambitious: to develop an "automated research scientist" that can do research on its own.

## What does it want to do?

As the company describes it, this system will ask questions, form hypotheses, design experiments, analyze results and revise its assumptions when needed, all by itself. The aim is not black-box predictions in narrow fields, but interpretable new knowledge. The work is grouped under six headings: multi-agent orchestration, pretraining, continual learning, reinforcement learning, systems and evaluations.

The evaluations heading stands out in particular. The team wants to build new tests that measure not what models have memorized, but their ability to do research and generalize beyond what is known. Proposing more efficient architectures and synthesizing data that enables broader generalization are also among the example questions.

## Open publishing and computing power

Perplexity says it will publish its findings openly as papers, blog posts, data, models and code. According to AI Weekly, the unit shared 19 example arXiv papers to start with and named NYU professor Andrew Gordon Wilson as research lead. The work is backed by more than 10,000 GPUs. The team is accepting applications for staff and a fellowship program in San Francisco, Palo Alto, New York and London. The announcement does not yet include a release date for the first system or any externally verified result.

## Why does it matter?

The race to turn AI from a tool that only answers questions into a partner that advances science on its own is speeding up. While most labs in this field keep their results closed, Perplexity is putting open publishing front and center; the first publications will show how well the promise is kept.
