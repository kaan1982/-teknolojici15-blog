Singapore-based cybersecurity company CloudSEK revealed the details of an espionage operation in a report published on 11 November. According to the company, a hacker group called "Silent Lynx" stole more than 2,000 documents related to national security from Tajikistan's government systems. Most of the work was done by an AI agent.

## What did the agent do?

The attackers used Hermes, an open-source autonomous agent framework. According to CloudSEK, the agent followed its instructions to plan the attack, wrote the rules and programs it needed, ran them, kept notes and reported to its operators. The instructions asked the agent to see itself as an "elite offensive security expert".

Once inside, the agent also wrote a custom program that downloaded the documents, organised them and split them into small pieces to make them easier to move out.

## How did it come to light?

The attackers accidentally left one of their servers open to the internet. This let researchers see the instructions given to the agent, its working rules and how the agent communicated with its operators. Reconnaissance began in mid-August; about two weeks later, the attackers got into Tajikistan's electronic document management system.

The group reportedly speaks Russian, works during Kazakhstan office hours and often accessed the server from a home connection in Kazakhstan. These clues alone do not prove state backing. Officials in Kazakhstan and Tajikistan did not comment at first.

## Why it matters

AI agents no longer just help write code; they can carry out the steps of an attack from start to finish. That means even small teams can run large operations. The lesson for defenders is clear too: the logs and mistakes agents leave behind can give attackers away, as they did here.
