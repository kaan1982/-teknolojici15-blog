SpaceX is targeting Friday, 13 November for the 15th test flight of its giant Starship rocket. The date was announced on 5 November at the UP.Summit event by Kiko Dontchev, the company's vice president of launch. In a post on 6 November, Elon Musk also said that this flight will attempt to catch both the Super Heavy booster and the Starship ship.

## Why is it a first?

SpaceX has already caught the Super Heavy booster in mid-air several times with the launch tower's mechanical arms, nicknamed "chopsticks"; the first time was on the 5th flight in October 2024. The upper stage ship, however, has never been caught. On every previous flight, the ship came down in the ocean. According to Musk, if both catches succeed, every part of a rocket that reached orbit will have returned to Earth intact for the first time.

Bringing the ship back from orbital speed and steering it to the tower is much harder than catching the booster. To do this, the ship has to follow a reentry path toward land instead of the open ocean.

## Vehicles ready, approval pending

According to Teslarati, the flight will use Booster 22 and Ship 42. The booster completed a test on 25 October firing all 33 of its Raptor engines, and the ship rehearsed a catch with the tower's arms in September. At the time of writing, no license for the 15th flight appeared on the US Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) page. Weather, hardware or the approval process could push the date back.

The 14th flight at the end of September was the first in which Starship reached orbit, and it released 26 Starlink V3 satellites.
