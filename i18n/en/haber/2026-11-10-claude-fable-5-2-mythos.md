On 9 November, Anthropic announced new versions of its two most powerful models: Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2. With this launch ahead of its upcoming IPO, the company argues that it has overtaken GPT-6 Astra, which OpenAI released in September, on both performance and price.

## Price and access

Fable 5.2 costs $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens. That is 20 percent lower than the previous version, Fable 5.1, and GPT-6 Astra. An optional fast mode offers up to 2.5 times the speed at double the price. Fable 5.2 is available to everyone; besides Anthropic's own platform, it can also be used through Amazon Web Services, Google Cloud and Microsoft Azure.

Mythos 5.2, a version of the same base model with relaxed cybersecurity and biology restrictions, is available only by application, to approved cybersecurity professionals and life sciences researchers.

## The company's claimed results

According to Anthropic, a team of 1,000 Fable 5.2 agents converted the proof of Fermat's Last Theorem into a form a computer can check step by step in 30 hours. An unreleased research model had previously done the same job in 11 days. The company also says the model solved two open problems in black hole physics and designed three working electronic devices from a single-paragraph prompt. On the robot control benchmark RoboDojo, the score rose from 27.6 percent to 64.9 percent.

In safety tests, in scenarios with a deliberately planted vulnerability, the rate at which Mythos 5.2 reached for files or credentials outside its sandbox fell to 3.1 percent; for Mythos 5 this figure was 39 percent. Before release, the models were also tested by the UK AI Security Institute and METR.

## Why it matters

Most of the results are the company's own measurements and have not been independently verified. Even so, the price cut shows that competition among top-tier models now runs on price as much as on capability.
