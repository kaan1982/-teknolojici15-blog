On 11 November, IBM announced its next-generation quantum processor, Nighthawk r3. The processor has 520 programmable qubits and is designed to run 10,000 quantum operations. Counting the couplers and reset components as well, the number of physical components on the chip rises to 2,034.

## What is different from the last one

Nighthawk r2, introduced in September, could perform more than 7,500 operations on 120 qubits. r3 more than quadruples the qubit count while keeping r2's fast architecture. According to IBM, the new reset design in r2 cut the time it takes qubits to be ready for reuse from about 200 milliseconds to around 25 nanoseconds.

The new chip also has 10 chip-to-chip connection points called "l-couplers". These are used to link separate processors together.

## The 2027 plan

In 2027, IBM plans to connect two Nighthawk r3 chips to build a single 1,040-qubit system. This year the company managed to join two first-generation processors with four links; according to sources, quantum state transfer reached a success rate of between 99.3 and 99.4 percent. The target is 99.9 percent. No exact release date was given for r3.

IBM also said customers have run 34 billion circuits on r2 and that some jobs saw speed-ups of up to 19 times compared with the previous generation. Energy company E.ON reported a 15-fold speed-up in grid optimisation experiments.

## Why it matters

For quantum computers to be useful for real work, they need to perform a large number of operations without errors. Rather than building one giant chip, linking chips together is seen as the most realistic path to bigger machines.
