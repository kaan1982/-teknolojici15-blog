In an essay titled "Perplexity and Intelligence" published on November 22, OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki shared for the first time a metric the company has tracked internally for years. The proposal is simple but ambitious: AI labs should track progress with a common measure and agree not to cross a certain threshold.

## What is perplexity?

Perplexity shows how well a language model predicts the next piece of a text; the lower the value, the better the model. OpenAI took the measurement on a fixed dataset drawn from its own codebase. According to the published figures, GPT-3.5 sat at roughly 0.99 and GPT-4 at roughly 0.91. Models that reached around 0.76 began producing breakthrough results in mathematics. The company estimates that the next big leap in capabilities could arrive between 0.68 and 0.70.

## What is being proposed?

Pachocki wants the companies building frontier models to set shared rules for evaluation and transparency. More importantly, he proposes common limits based on perplexity and other metrics: these limits would not be crossed until our ability to understand and monitor AI behavior reaches a concrete milestone, for example until a tool is developed to replace chain-of-thought monitors.

According to Latent Space, Microsoft AI was among the first to answer the call: it ran its own MAI-2 Base model on OpenAI's dataset and shared a score of 0.819.

## Limitations

OpenAI lists the weak points itself: labs use different datasets, perplexity does not map directly onto any particular task, and while reasoning boosts capabilities, it does not lower this value. It is also unknown what the lowest achievable value is.

## Why does it matter?

For the first time, a company leading the AI race has put a concrete metric on the table to tell its rivals "let's slow down together." Measurement has always set the direction of technology: what calculation speed was in the era of [the 30-ton ENIAC](/yazi/eniac-30-ton/), perplexity may be today.
