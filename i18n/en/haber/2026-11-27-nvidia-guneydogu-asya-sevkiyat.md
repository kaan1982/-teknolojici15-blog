According to a Financial Times report on November 26 citing three sources, Nvidia told its server making partners that it will not approve deliveries to buyers in Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia. Companies such as Dell and Supermicro, which build Nvidia chips into their own servers and sell them, have also stopped shipping to these countries. It is not clear how long the pause will last.

## What does the rule target?

The decision follows an interim rule issued by the Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS), part of the US Commerce Department. The rule requires a license to export advanced AI chips to data centers in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil. The aim is to stop Chinese companies that are barred from buying these chips from reaching them by renting from cloud providers in other countries. US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer told CNBC that the goal is to cut off smuggling through Southeast Asia and remote access from data centers in third countries.

Cloud providers in the region have 90 days to apply for a license and prove that restricted companies are not among their customers. One executive described the situation to the FT as chaos and said no guidance had been given on how to apply.

## First effects

Banks froze a loan of about 500 million dollars to Singapore's Aolani, which planned to rent computing power to ByteDance. According to CNBC, an unusual number of Nvidia processors have appeared for sale on the secondhand market, including thousands of B300 chips set aside for data centers meant for Chinese tenants. This week the Commerce Department also moved to put Singapore's Megaspeed on its export blacklist.

## Why does it matter?

Southeast Asia is a key market for Nvidia: the company's Singapore unit booked 104 billion dollars in revenue in 2025 (a figure that also includes some sales from Taiwan and China recorded there). A long halt to shipments could slow data center investment in the region, and it shows that the chip war is now fought not only over sales but over rentals too.
