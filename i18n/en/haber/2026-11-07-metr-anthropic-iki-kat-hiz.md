METR, a Berkeley-based nonprofit that independently evaluates AI models, published a roughly 100-page report on Anthropic on 5 November. According to the report, the company is developing its models about twice as fast on well-defined tasks as it did a year ago. The range METR considers plausible is 1.3x to 3x.

## How was it measured?

Anthropic requested the assessment, but METR says the work was carried out independently and that no fee was paid between the two sides. Between 12 August and 29 October, the team reviewed the company's internal data and interviewed researchers. Progress was tracked with Anthropic's capability index, which combines a range of tests into a single score. By this index, the speed-up is 1.9x.

METR says it is confident the speed-up does not come from faster growth in the computing power used for training. In the organization's subjective estimate, about 80 percent of the current speed-up is the result of AI, meaning models helping the researchers. It also found no convincing bottleneck that would halt this trend over the next 12 months.

## Blacked-out pages

The most debated part of the report is the redaction. According to METR, Anthropic blacked out much of the evidence it found most informative, on the grounds that it could help competitors. As a result, the public cannot check the key evidence itself and largely has to rely on METR's judgment. AP reports that there were also disagreements within the team; the results could be overstated or understated.

The report arrived just before Anthropic's initial public offering, planned for later this month. The company's CEO Dario Amodei called on the industry in September to slow the pace of capability gains and proposed placing outside auditors at the major labs.
