The US Department of Justice announced on 5 November that it had set up a new unit called the "Super Intelligence Task Force" (DOJ-SITF). The unit's job is to handle criminal and civil cases against the illegal use of advanced AI from a single place. According to the department's statement, its targets include foreign states and the groups they back, transnational criminal organizations, and people or companies committing fraud.

## Who will lead it?

The task force is headed by Attorney General Todd Blanche or his designees, together with Associate Deputy Attorneys General Stanley Woodward and Stephen Weiss. According to ExecutiveGov, the unit was created by a memo from Blanche dated 13 October. Representatives of units such as the FBI, the National Security Division and the Antitrust Division will also have a seat at the table.

The new body absorbs the department's existing AI task force, set up in January; its open cases and staff are being transferred too. The unit will also be the main point of contact between the department and the federal "Super Intelligence Force", created by President Trump's executive order of 29 September.

## What will change?

Prosecutors must notify the task force when asked, when an AI-related investigation begins, and before charges are filed. In emergencies, for example when there is an imminent threat to public safety, this step can be skipped. The unit will prosecute AI-enabled schemes targeting taxpayers, critical infrastructure or national security, assess companies' internal compliance programs, and prepare guidance on how to prosecute AI crimes. According to ExecutiveGov, when commenting on bills, the unit also aims not to stifle innovation with heavy regulation.

## Why it matters

Until now, AI has mostly been a subject of regulatory debate. The US is now gathering its prosecutorial power in this area into a single center.
