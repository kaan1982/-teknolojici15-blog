Attacks targeting supermarket refrigeration in Europe have reached a second wave. According to a roundup by The Guardian, on the night of Monday, 2 November, supermarket chains in Belgium, Estonia, Ireland, Finland, Luxembourg, the Netherlands and Sweden reported that the temperature of their fridges and freezers had suddenly risen. In some stores the settings had been pushed up to 50 degrees.

## Empty shelves

As TechCrunch reported, citing Reuters, Ahold Delhaize and Colruyt confirmed the attacks. A manager at Sabcobel, the Belgian company that runs Delhaize's refrigeration systems, said the attackers had switched the devices into defrost mode. Dozens of Delhaize stores in Belgium and Luxembourg were affected; Carrefour Belgium had problems in about 15 of its 700 stores. In Ireland, SuperValu pulled chilled products from sale in 13 of its 223 stores, and in Sweden, Coop said six of its stores had been hit.

Christophe Hansen, the EU commissioner for agriculture and food, described the attacks as a deliberate attempt to disrupt the food supply chain. The next day, the Guardian reported that some food suppliers in the UK had also been hit by similar attacks.

## It started in France

In mid-October, the refrigeration systems of up to 700 supermarkets in France were taken over, with losses measured in tens of millions of euros. Most of the control devices used in that attack were made by Denmark's Danfoss. Security firm Claroty had warned in August that these devices could be hijacked remotely; Danfoss says it identified the vulnerabilities last year and recommended updates to its customers. No one has claimed the attacks, and there has been no ransom demand. According to a researcher at SentinelOne, this picture points to sabotage rather than money.

## Why it matters

Any internet-connected device can cause real-world damage if it is not updated. Here, what was lost was not stolen data but meat, milk and frozen food rotting overnight. Sweden's cybersecurity centre says there are many exposed industrial devices in the country that need updating.
