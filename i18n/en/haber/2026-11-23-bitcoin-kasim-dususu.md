Bitcoin is heading for its worst November since 2018. According to Coinglass data, the cryptocurrency has lost more than 20 percent since the start of the month. On November 21, the intraday low was measured between $55,081 and $55,252, depending on the source. A recovery came over the weekend, and the price climbed back above $60,000 on November 22.

## Where did the money go?

The drop was accompanied by heavy institutional selling. According to SoSoValue data, a net $4.14 billion flowed out of US spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds (ETFs) in November. That is the largest monthly loss since the $4.51 billion outflow in June this year. The funds lost about $1.8 billion over four straight trading days up to November 20. As reported by CoinDesk, $2.4 billion worth of positions in crypto futures were forcibly closed on November 20.

Analysts link the selling to the slide in tech and AI stocks, expectations of rate hikes and rising real yields. The "Fear and Greed Index", which measures market sentiment, sits between 11 and 18, in the "extreme fear" zone. Bitcoin has fallen to almost half of the roughly $126,000 peak it reached in October 2025.

## The four-year cycle debate

Some investors explain the drop, which comes almost exactly four years after the 2022 bottom, with the "four-year cycle" theory. Institutions such as Fidelity, however, stress that this cycle is not a reliable tool for timing the market.

## Why does it matter?

Spot ETFs were presented as the main gateway for institutional money into Bitcoin. That the same gateway is being used just as fast for the exit shows that crypto is still tightly tied to risk appetite in the wider market.
