Physical Superintelligence (PSI), a young research company in Cambridge in the US, announced that its AI-assisted research system had proved the Aaronson-Ambainis conjecture, one of the important open problems in quantum computing. The system ran on multiple copies of an Anthropic model that is not publicly available. The news has been covered in the science and technology press since 6 November.

## What does the conjecture say?

The conjecture, put forward by Scott Aaronson and Andris Ambainis in 2008, asks whether quantum computers can offer an exponential advantage over classical computers on random inputs. According to the new proof, for most random inputs a classical algorithm can approximate the probability that a quantum algorithm says "yes" without an exponential increase. In other words, a large quantum speedup requires structure in the input.

## How was the proof verified?

According to PSI, the first full proof appeared on the third day of the system's run. The model copies then worked on related questions and improved the result twice. The main theorem and the statement of the conjecture were formalized and accepted in Lean, software that checks mathematical proofs step by step. PSI researcher David Turturean, who led the work, chose the problem and reviewed and edited the draft written by the model.

Stanford researchers Guy Blanc, Jordan Docter, Carmen Strassle and Li-Yang Tan, who had proved a special case of the conjecture in August, are reading the proof and preparing a short explanation for experts. The group described the result as a major advance. However, there is no independent peer review yet.

## Why it matters

The result measures how many times the input is read, not total computation time; so it does not deliver a general verdict on the commercial value of quantum computers. Still, it is one of the rare examples of AI solving a real open mathematical problem with machine-checked proofs.
