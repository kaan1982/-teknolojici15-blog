Hackers have breached Cloud One, the US Air Force's largest cloud computing platform. According to a Reuters report on November 27 citing three sources familiar with the matter, the attack was discovered within the past month while the system was being updated. Who the attackers are, the full extent of the breach and whether any data was stolen are not yet known.

The Air Force said it does not comment on the security or operational status of specific networks and that it continually takes measures to protect its information.

## What does Cloud One do?

Launched in 2017, Cloud One is a "one stop shop" platform that brings the infrastructure of Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle and Alphabet under one roof. Many applications run on it, from personnel and payroll records to logistics and maintenance systems. Among them are the command and control system that collects air defense radar data across North America and the munitions system that tracks bombs and missiles at more than 200 bases worldwide.

One of the sources said the attackers used a software vulnerability to reach the platform's control layer, likening it to a "universal remote". With that access, the status of aircraft or the movement of spare parts could be seen, but it is unclear whether this was done. US Cyber Command has been brought in to assess the damage.

## The impact of the attack

After the breach was discovered, the migration of back office applications to the platform was halted, updates to existing applications were blocked and some users had their administrator privileges removed. The platform's prime contractor changed from SAIC to Leidos in 2025; Leidos referred questions to the Air Force.

The incident is the latest in a series of attacks in recent weeks that have also hit the FBI, the Treasury Department and two intelligence agencies. Experts point out that the ability of AI models to quickly find weak spots in large cloud systems is raising the risk. ## Why does it matter?

Gathering military systems on a single cloud platform brings efficiency, but it also shows that a single vulnerability can put a very wide area at risk. Cloud One's managers boasted in 2023 of a "100 percent success" rate in repelling attacks; this breach is a reminder that even the best protected clouds can be vulnerable during updates.
