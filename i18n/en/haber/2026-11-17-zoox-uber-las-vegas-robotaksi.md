Zoox, Amazon's self-driving vehicle company, started carrying passengers through the Uber app in Las Vegas on November 16. This is the first time Zoox has partnered with a third-party platform outside its own app.

## How does it work?

For now, riders cannot choose Zoox directly. People requesting UberX, Uber Comfort, Uber Electric, Uber Comfort Electric or Wait & Save can be matched with a Zoox robotaxi at no extra charge. Those who turn on autonomous rides in the app's ride preferences have a better chance of being matched. The service area covers hotels and venues around the Strip, downtown and the Arts District. Zoox also continues to offer rides through its own app.

Zoox vehicles do not look like ordinary cars: in these purpose-built vehicles, passengers sit facing each other. Uber, Zoox and MGM Resorts also announced a multi-year deal on the same day; MGM hotels will get easier pickup points and wayfinding signs for robotaxis.

## What comes next?

Earlier this year, Nevada's transportation authority gave Uber permission to operate up to 1,000 robotaxis in Clark County through its Motional and Zoox partnerships. Uber CEO Dara Khosrowshahi had said Las Vegas would be the first US city where riders can access two separate fully driverless partners through Uber. Zoox plans to offer rides via Uber in Los Angeles as well in 2027. According to TechCrunch, Uber will not handle maintenance of Zoox vehicles, a different model from its other autonomous vehicle partnerships.

## Why does it matter?

Waymo and Tesla lead the robotaxi race. For Zoox, reaching Uber's millions of users is the fastest way to reach riders who would never download its own app. Uber, for its part, aims to become a marketplace that brings several autonomous vehicle makers together in one app without depending on a single company.
