The Canadian government has announced a new phase of the AI Compute Access Fund, which covers the cost of the computing power small and medium sized companies need for AI. 700 million Canadian dollars were added to the fund, bringing its total size to 1 billion Canadian dollars. Evan Solomon, Minister of Artificial Intelligence and Digital Innovation, made the announcement in Toronto on November 26.

## What do the new rules bring?

The fund supports projects with compute costs between 100,000 and 5 million Canadian dollars. If a company buys its cloud service from a "sovereign" provider, meaning one that is owned, operated and controlled in Canada, the government pays up to 80 percent of eligible costs. For other providers located in Canada, the rate is 50 percent. According to BetaKit, the rate for Canadian providers was 67 percent in the previous round.

Canadian companies with fewer than 500 employees that generate revenue or have raised a Series A round and are developing AI products can benefit from the fund. The first call for applications focuses on health and life sciences and is open until January 4, 2027.

## Demand exceeded expectations

The fund opened in March 2025 with 300 million Canadian dollars. Solomon said that while a few hundred applications were expected, about 1,500 requests came in. The minister also announced more than 52 million Canadian dollars in support for 45 companies, bringing total commitments to over 118 million Canadian dollars for 89 companies. In its 2026 budget, the government had also promised 500 megawatts of sovereign AI compute capacity. ## Why does it matter?

The real bottleneck in the AI race is computing power, and big cloud companies hold most of that capacity. Canada is trying to give small companies cheap compute while steering demand toward domestic infrastructure. This "sovereign AI" approach could also become a model for other countries that want to invest in their own data centers.
