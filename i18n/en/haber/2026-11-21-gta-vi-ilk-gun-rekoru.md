On November 20, Rockstar Games announced that first-day retail sales of Grand Theft Auto VI had reached 3.79 billion dollars. With that figure, the company says the game is the biggest launch not only in gaming but in the entire entertainment industry, film and music included.

## What do the numbers say?

According to the press release Take-Two Interactive filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), the game sold 32.5 million copies before launch, a new pre-order record across all platforms. The sales figure is based on the company's worldwide estimate through the end of the day on November 19. The game opened with a score of 99 on Metacritic.

For comparison, CNBC points out that GTA V, released in 2013, earned around 800 million dollars on its first day. In other words, the new game appears to have beaten its predecessor's first day by more than four times. According to the report, Take-Two spent more than 1 billion dollars on making and marketing the game. Investors are now looking at the PC version expected in 2027 and at the online mode, which is where most of the long-term revenue is expected to come from.

One note: 3.79 billion dollars is retail sales, meaning the total amount players paid in stores. Take-Two's own revenue will be lower once store and platform cuts are taken out.

## Why does it matter?

In the shadow of AI news, the gaming industry has shown once again how much money a single product can generate in one day. GTA VI launching only on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S also directly affects console sales and digital stores. The record is also a strong answer to the debate over whether big-budget game development is still profitable.
