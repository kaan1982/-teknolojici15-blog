German software company SAP is preparing to eliminate at least 10,000 positions to rebuild its business for the AI era. According to a Bloomberg report on November 26 citing people familiar with the matter, the plan would affect at least 9 percent of its employees worldwide. The number is not final yet and could change after talks with employee representatives.

SAP did not answer the question of whether there will be layoffs; it only said it constantly reviews its organization to stay aligned with its strategy and customer needs.

## Which divisions are targeted?

According to the report, all divisions were asked to identify areas where cuts could be made, with product, engineering and sales in particular focus. CEO Christian Klein is trying to move the company to an "AI first" strategy and wants to build AI tools into SAP applications. The executive board was reshuffled twice this year: two of its six members leave in March, and sales chief Thomas Saueressig has announced he is leaving to become CEO of Ionos.

## The second big wave in three years

SAP also carried out a restructuring in 2024 that affected about 10,000 positions. That program, which cost roughly 3 to 3.1 billion euros, ran largely through voluntary departures and retraining; because the company also hired new people, total headcount did not fall, and it ended 2025 with 111,397 employees. In Germany, the works council secured an employment guarantee this year that runs until the end of 2028, which could make cuts harder. CFO Dominik Asam said last week that he would be very surprised if the growth in headcount did not reverse.

## Why does it matter?

The cut plan does not come from bad results: SAP reported revenue above 10 billion euros for the first time in the third quarter, and cloud revenue rose 26 percent. A profitable company discussing shrinking by tens of thousands of people and citing AI as the reason is a strong signal of how white collar software jobs will take shape from here on.
