US drone delivery company Zipline announced on 5 November that it had closed a new $1.5 billion funding round. The round lifts the company's valuation to $21.5 billion. According to Tech Startups, Zipline raised more than $600 million at a $7.6 billion valuation in January, so its value has nearly tripled in about 10 months.

## Who invested?

The round was led by Paradigm, WCM Investment Management and Layer Global. Fidelity, Uber, Tiger Global, J.P. Morgan, ARK Invest, Valor Equity Partners and Salesforce Ventures also took part. Zipline will use the money to expand its US manufacturing, hire staff and build a nationwide drone delivery network.

## Growth with Walmart and Uber

According to the company's announcement, a new multi-year deal with Walmart covers 300 additional delivery sites. Uber, together with Zipline, aims to reach 1 million drone deliveries a day by the end of 2029. This year Zipline made more than 1 million deliveries in the Dallas-Fort Worth area alone. In October the average time from order to door was 19 minutes; with traditional delivery it is over 30 minutes.

Zipline's aircraft hover about 100 meters up and lower the package on a tether. The company says it has completed about 240 million kilometers (150 million miles) of commercial autonomous flight without a serious safety incident. Tech Startups stresses that this figure comes from the company's own reporting, not an independent audit.

## Why it matters

Zipline started out in Africa, carrying blood and medicine to hospitals, and it still serves more than 5,000 health facilities today. Now it is bringing the same technology to everyday grocery and food orders. The size of the investment shows strong expectations that drone delivery will move beyond the trial stage and become an everyday service.
