On November 23, Nvidia's board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as president, CEO and board member. The decision took effect immediately. Huang had resigned only 11 days earlier from the company he founded in 1993.

## Why did he leave, and why is he back?

Huang stepped down after his relationship with a woman whom US intelligence suspected of spying for China came to light. China denies the allegation. According to the company's statement, after a series of conversations with Huang, the board became confident in his integrity and his ability to lead the company. The board also said it had consulted the highest levels of the US government and was confident that Huang's return posed no national security risk.

The return came as a surprise: according to Axios, board chair Harvey Jones had said only six days earlier that there were no plans to bring Huang back. President Trump, meanwhile, openly pressed the board for his return and applauded the decision on social media. Interim CEO Colette Kress returns to her role as chief financial officer.

## Markets and Congress

On the news, Nvidia's stock rose more than 30 percent. According to CBS News, the return lifted the whole market, and the Nasdaq quickly erased the losses from Friday's sharp sell-off.

It has not gone without criticism: according to Axios, lawmakers from both parties are criticizing the decision, and pressure continues for Huang to disclose overseas chip sales. The company announced that the independent investigation into Huang will continue to its conclusion, that it has created a new National Security and Export Control Committee, and that it is searching for a Chief Compliance Officer.

## Why does it matter?

Nvidia is the chipmaker at the center of AI investment, and the departure of a single executive was enough to shake the entire stock market.
