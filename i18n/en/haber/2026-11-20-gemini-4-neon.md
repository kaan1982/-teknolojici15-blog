On the evening of November 18, Google announced Gemini 4 Neon, the newest member of the Gemini 4 family. The company is pitching the model as an option that delivers "frontier-level intelligence quickly and affordably" for complex software tasks, analysis and scientific workflows.

## Where does it fit in the family?

The Gemini 4 family now has three main tiers: Hydrogen for everyday tasks, Carbon for the hardest and longest-running jobs, and Neon in between. According to 9to5Google, Neon replaces Argon, which was offered for code and analysis, and arrives in the Gemini app as the "Deeper analysis" option for AI Pro and Ultra subscribers. The model works with a 1 million token context window and four thinking levels.

## Price and performance

Neon costs 3 dollars per million input tokens and 15 dollars per million output tokens. For input read from the cache, the price drops by 95 percent to 0.15 dollars. According to measurements shared by Google, at its highest thinking level the model ranks third overall on the Artificial Analysis intelligence index with 61 points, and first on the same organization's coding agent index. Google says that at the default setting the cost per task is 32 percent lower than Carbon's. Most of these results come from Google's own announcement; independent tests are only just starting to appear.

The model is available through the Gemini API, Google AI Studio, enterprise Gemini and Google Antigravity. A version tuned for cybersecurity will also open in the coming days to selected defense teams in the Fairwind program.

## Why does it matter?

In the AI race, what counts is no longer only "the smartest model" but also "the model that does the smartest model's job for less". As companies run agents all day long, the token bill keeps growing; mid-tier models like Neon arrive promising to cut that cost. Google releasing Carbon, Hydrogen and Neon one after another this month also shows how fast the competition is moving.
