Anthropic, the developer of the Claude models, began trading on Nasdaq under the ticker "ANT" on November 19. A day earlier, the company had priced its initial public offering at 83 dollars per share, above the range of 70 to 78 dollars announced at the start of the month.

## A record IPO

Anthropic sold a total of 980 million Class A shares; roughly 956 million of them came directly from the company and the rest from existing shareholders. The earlier plan called for 825 million shares, so strong demand pushed up both the price and the size of the offering. The 81.3 billion dollars raised overtook SpaceX, which raised 75 billion dollars in June, making it the largest IPO in US history. Depending on the calculation method, the company's IPO valuation is between 1.4 and 1.5 trillion dollars.

## A bumpy first day

According to CBS News, the stock opened at 111.50 dollars and climbed as high as 114.99 dollars during the day. Some of those gains were given back by the close, and the stock ended at 97.17 dollars, about 17 percent above the IPO price. The Nasdaq index fell more than 2 percent the same day; Anthropic was one of the few big names to rise in a falling market.

Anthropic thus became the first major "frontier" AI lab to go public. The company is not yet profitable and, according to its prospectus, has committed to spending about 1 trillion dollars on chips and infrastructure through 2045.

## Why does it matter?

The valuations of AI companies have been debated for months. Anthropic's IPO showed for the first time, on an open market, how much money investors are still willing to put into the field. From now on, each of the company's quarterly reports will be a public report card on whether the AI business can really turn a profit.
