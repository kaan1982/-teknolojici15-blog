On 15 November, Apple launched a virtual research study in the US called "Apple Health AI Usage Study". An app developed for the study together with clinical AI company OpenEvidence has been put on the App Store. The goal is to measure, with up to 200,000 volunteers, how people ask AI their health questions and how much they trust the answers they get.

## What will participants do?

Participants will ask the app everyday health questions, get complex medical information explained in plain language, and review the links to peer-reviewed medical sources given with each answer. According to TechCrunch, the answers draw on sources such as the New England Journal of Medicine, JAMA and Nature. Volunteers will rate the answers, take monthly surveys and, if they wish, share Health data such as resting heart rate, activity, sleep or lab results to get more personal answers. The app also helps people prepare for a doctor's appointment.

To take part you need to live in the US, be over 21 and use iOS 26 or later on an iPhone that supports Apple Intelligence (iPhone 15 Pro and later). TechCrunch writes that the study will run for more than 12 months.

## Privacy and limits

Apple says study data will not be used to train AI models. According to TechCrunch, the data is anonymised and sent to a cloud provider, and OpenEvidence deletes questions and answers after 24 hours. The app is not meant to diagnose or recommend treatment; it includes safeguards that direct the user to a health professional for worrying questions.

## Why does it matter?

Millions of people already ask chatbots health questions, but there is little large-scale data on how they do it and how much they trust the results. Whether Apple will bring these findings to the Health app or Siri in the future is not yet clear.
