On 3 November, Apple took a big step for third-party developers on Siri AI. As 9to5Mac reported, every app can now build its own Siri AI integration for the first time. The new Siri, which arrived in beta in September, had until now worked only with a limited number of ready-made templates.

## What will app skills do?

The new feature is called "app skills." With it, developers can teach Siri how to carry out complex, multi-step tasks in their apps. In the example from Apple's developer documentation, the user asks Siri to send the sneakers they added to their favourites to their partner through a messaging app. Siri finds the shoes in one app, works out who the partner is from Contacts and sends the information through the other app.

Most of the time, users will not even need to say the app's name. For example, if a ride-hailing app's Live Activity is open on screen, Siri will work out on its own that the command "change the ride destination" belongs to that app. The features are being tested in the developer betas of iOS 27.3, macOS 27.3 and their companion releases that are out now. According to Observer, this is an early developer preview; App Store submissions will open in the spring.

## Big numbers, and big costs

Apple CEO John Ternus said on the 2 November earnings call that Siri answers more than 2.5 billion requests a day; that figure does not break out the new AI features separately. As Observer reported, Apple expects its gross margin to slip to between 45 and 46 percent for the December quarter. The main reason is memory prices, but Siri AI's cloud costs also play a part.

## Why it matters

An assistant's value is measured by the number of things it can do. Limiting Siri to Apple's own apps was leaving it behind rivals' agents. A Siri that links apps together could effectively kick off the "agent" era on the iPhone.
