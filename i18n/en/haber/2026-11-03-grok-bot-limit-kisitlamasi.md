Users of Elon Musk's AI agent Grok Bot ran into something strange over the Halloween weekend: their weekly usage limits suddenly filled up even though they had not run the agent at all. Some users wrote that while they slept, their limits jumped from 50 percent or 70 percent to 100 percent.

## Not a bug, a decision

Replying to a user's question on X, Musk confirmed on Sunday, November 1, that this was not a malfunction. According to Musk, the cost of running Grok Bot was unsustainably high compared with the revenue it generated. TechCrunch notes that Musk never addressed the issue in his own main feed and did not explain how the limits will change in the future.

Grok Bot is not an ordinary chatbot. As Tesla North reports, the agent runs in the background on its own virtual computer, doing research, writing text and browsing the web. Tasks like these consume far more computing power than a simple chat reply, and usage limits exist precisely to keep that cost in check.

The user reaction was harsh. According to TechRadar, one Reddit thread described the change as an "indirect rug pull". What the new limits are, whether plan prices will go up and whether affected users will have their limits reset all remain unclear.

## Why does it matter?

AI agents run far longer than a single question, and the bill grows accordingly. Companies start out with generous limits to attract users, then cut back as costs climb. The Grok Bot case clearly shows that the era of "unlimited" AI is expensive. Rationing expensive computer time with quotas is not a new idea: we described what happened at MIT in the 1960s when one researcher's weekly quota was not enough in our article on [the first computer password](/yazi/ilk-bilgisayar-sifresi-ctss/).
