On 13 November, OpenAI rolled out a payment feature called ChatGPT Wallet so that AI agents can shop on users' behalf. The feature works with OpenAI's "dots" agents, which run continuously, learn the user's preferences and keep track of long-running tasks.

## How does it work?

The user adds their card once. Before each purchase, they see the items, the total amount and the selected card, and approve it with a security check such as Face ID or a fingerprint. Each approval creates a record of what the agent is authorised to buy. The card network then generates a payment token that stands in for the real card, with its own number, security code and expiry date. That way, neither the merchant nor the agent sees the real card details. Card rewards and, where applicable, dispute rights are preserved.

The wallet was developed together with American Express, Mastercard and Visa. Forter, Riskified, Sardine and Signifyd are partners on fraud prevention, and Adyen, Checkout.com and Stripe on payments. OpenAI reportedly does not take a commission on purchases for now.

## Who can use it?

At launch, only ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the US who are over 18 and have an eligible US-issued card. OpenAI says that in the future users will be able to hand shopping over to the agent entirely by setting limits on spending amount, category or time, but it has not given a date. The feature does not work on sites that block agents; some large companies already prevent agents from making purchases or moving money.

## Why it matters

Spending money is one of the most critical thresholds for AI agents to get things done in the real world. The token model, which hides card details from the agent completely, is one of the first major attempts, backed by the payment networks themselves, to cross that threshold safely.
