At the TomorrowX summit in Austin on November 18, Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan announced that the company had signed its first external customer for 18A, its most advanced manufacturing process. Tan did not name the customer. Intel technology executive Pushkar Ranade later clarified on X that the deal is for the enhanced 18A-P process, which entered risk production in June.

## Why is this such a big step?

For years Intel has manufactured only its own processors. Building a foundry business that also makes other companies' chips is at the heart of its plan to compete with Taiwan's TSMC. Until now, this business had no well-known external customer. Analyst Ben Bajarin, quoted by CNBC, says the customer could be Nvidia, Apple or one of the big cloud companies that design their own processors; these companies are struggling to find enough capacity at TSMC.

## Yield at 70 percent

Tan also said manufacturing yield on 18A has reached 70 percent. When he took the job in March 2025, that yield was described as "terrible". Hardware Busters notes that Tan did not specify which kind of yield he meant, and that it is most likely the share of working dies. 18A is currently used in Panther Lake laptop processors and Clearwater Forest server chips.

On the same day, Intel shared a second piece of good news: Microsoft became the first announced customer to use Intel's EMIB packaging technology for the chips it designs itself for Azure. The deal does not include a product name, volume or value.

## Why does it matter?

Most of the world's most advanced chips are made by a single company, TSMC. Intel winning external customers could break up some of that concentration and give concrete backing to the goal of chips made in the US. We explained in [our Intel 4004 article](/yazi/intel-4004-ilk-mikroislemci/) that Intel began this journey in 1971 with a single small processor.
