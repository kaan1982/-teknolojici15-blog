On 5 November, Huawei unveiled the HM650 desktop computer series at its HarmonyOS office summit in Wuhan. As reported by the South China Morning Post, the company says these are China's first "100 percent domestic" desktop computers and that every component, from the processor to the memory modules, is made in China.

## What's inside?

The series consists of the HM650 for businesses and the HM650g for government agencies. The computers use Huawei's own Kirin X90 processor; the most powerful configuration offers 10 cores and 20 threads. The processor's 40-watt thermal design power is below the 65 to 125 watts of mainstream desktop processors. The machines have LPDDR5 memory, SSD storage and a dedicated key for the AI assistant Celia.

The operating system is not Windows but the enterprise edition of Huawei's HarmonyOS. The computers will be sold in China through enterprise and government channels; no plans for overseas sales have been announced. Some local listings indicate that sales will begin on 1 December.

## A result of sanctions

In 2024, the US revoked the licenses that allowed Intel and Qualcomm to sell processors to Huawei. Since then, Huawei has replaced both the processor and the operating system, first in its laptops and now in its desktops. The company said it had sold more than 1 million HarmonyOS computers as of October.

Still, the SCMP notes that the scope of the "100 percent domestic" certification is unclear. The certification refers to the source of the hardware; it does not mean that all the technology and intellectual property was developed in China. For now, the performance claims also rest only on Huawei's own tests.

## Why it matters

The processor and the operating system, the two cornerstones of a computer, have been largely in the hands of US companies for decades. China's ability to supply fully domestic machines to its public institutions shows that chip sanctions could split the tech world into two separate ecosystems.
