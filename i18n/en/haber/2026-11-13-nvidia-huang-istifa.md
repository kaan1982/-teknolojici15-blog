Jensen Huang, co-founder of Nvidia and its leader since 1993, has resigned as CEO. According to a regulatory filing, the board accepted the resignation on Wednesday, 11 November, and the company announced the decision on Thursday, 12 November. Huang also left the board, ending a 33-year run. Nvidia did not give a reason for the departure.

## New leadership

CFO Colette Kress will serve as interim CEO until a permanent successor is found, and she will keep her CFO role as well. Kress has held that position since 2013; over that period Nvidia's annual revenue grew from 4.13 billion dollars to 215.9 billion dollars. Harvey Jones, who has been on the board since 1993, became the company's first chairman. The board is considering candidates for the new CEO from both inside and outside the company.

## What happened in one week?

Last Sunday, Huang admitted to a mistake in his personal life. The same day, the board launched an independent review following reports that Huang had been targeted by Chinese intelligence. On Wednesday, new reports emerged that Huang had a relationship with a senior executive who reports to him, raising questions about a conflict of interest. The board says it sees no reason to believe national security was put at risk; there is no established evidence that Huang knowingly helped a foreign state or that company secrets were leaked.

Shares fell more than 11 percent in pre-market trading on Thursday, and at one point during the day the loss exceeded 14 percent. The company's market value has shrunk by more than 300 billion dollars since last Friday.

## Why it matters

Nvidia's chips are the backbone of today's AI infrastructure. The departure of a leader who has run the company since its founding and who is the face of its product strategy creates major uncertainty, both for investors and for the chip rivalry between the US and China.
