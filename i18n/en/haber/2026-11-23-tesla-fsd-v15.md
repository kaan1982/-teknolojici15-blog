On November 21, Tesla announced that it had started rolling out v15 of its driver assistance system "Full Self-Driving" (FSD). According to the company, the update renews every part of the end-to-end driving neural network. The first builds are going to members of the Early Access Program; Elon Musk said the wide release will come in December.

## What is changing?

The biggest claim in the release notes concerns a new vision encoder shared by FSD, Robotaxi and the humanoid robot Optimus. This layer, trained on millions of internet videos, is said to improve the rate of critical interventions, where the driver has to step in, by 25 times. Electrek points out that Tesla only shared the multiplier and did not disclose actual mileage data.

The most eye-catching new feature, however, is "Reality View". The center screen draws the street around the car as a much more detailed, live image, with the aim of letting the driver see and steer what FSD is planning to do. Traffic signs, small animals, road debris and potholes are also detected better; speed management in school zones improves, and the car slows down at speed cameras.

One change could prove controversial: Tesla says it has relaxed driver attention monitoring in high-speed driving profiles.

## How reliable is it?

According to Electrek, almost every improvement on the list targets FSD's most criticized weak spots today: misread speed limits, potholes and school zones. Still, past versions of FSD brought both progress and setbacks, and the system still requires the driver's constant supervision.

## Why does it matter?

This is one of the biggest experiments in which AI leaves the screen and enters millions of cars on real roads. If the 25x claim holds up, driverless taxis could speed up; if not, pressure from regulators could grow.
