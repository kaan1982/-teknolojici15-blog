AI stocks were hit by a sharp sell-off on US markets on Thursday, 5 November. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite fell 5.5% and entered "correction" territory, meaning it is now at least 10% below its latest peak. The S&P 500 dropped about 4.4%, and the Dow Jones lost 1,833 points (3.4%). All three indexes had their worst day since the tariff shock of April 2025.

## A day in numbers

According to figures compiled by CNBC, the seven tech giants known as the "Magnificent Seven" lost more than $1 trillion in market value combined. The biggest share came from Nvidia, which shed more than $440 billion; the stock fell over 9% and is now down for the year. The S&P 500's information technology sector dropped 7.3%, its worst day since March 2020.

According to Yahoo Finance, the SOX semiconductor index fell more than 12%, and the Nasdaq-100 lost $2.3 trillion in value in one day. AMD fell about 15% during the session and Micron around 12%. AI software companies such as Palantir and CrowdStrike were also caught up in the sell-off.

## Why the drop?

Investors' main worry was that Democrats, who won Congress in the midterm elections, would put new obstacles in the way of data center investment. Voters rejecting data centers in many local ballots fed that fear. As Yahoo Finance reported, Nomura's Charlie McElligott believes the sell-off came less from fundamentals than from funds taking profits before the end of the year. Retail investors also had one of their biggest net selling days since 2020. The post-election rally of the day before was wiped out completely.

## Why it matters

AI investment has been the engine of the stock market for the past two years. The expectation that data center construction will slow is hitting the whole chain at once, from chipmakers to cloud companies. Earnings reports in the coming weeks will show whether this is a lasting turn or a short-lived panic.
