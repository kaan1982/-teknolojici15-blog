For the first time, SpaceX has caught the ship, the upper stage of its giant Starship rocket, in mid-air with the mechanical arms of its launch tower after a return from orbit. Starship's 15th test flight lifted off from the Starbase facility in Texas on the afternoon of Friday, 13 November. About 22.5 hours later, at around 3:20 p.m. local time on Saturday, 14 November (just after midnight on 15 November in Istanbul), the ship returned to the tower.

## What happened during the flight?

The ship reached orbit and deployed 26 third-generation Starlink satellites. Some of these satellites photographed the ship's heat shield before reentry, allowing engineers to examine its condition. The ship completed 14 orbits, then relit a single Raptor engine to leave orbit. Entering the atmosphere at roughly 28,000 kilometres per hour, it performed a final turning manoeuvre over the Gulf of Mexico, fired three engines to swing upright and settled between the two arms of the tower known as "Mechazilla".

## Why wasn't the booster caught?

The plan called for the Super Heavy booster to be caught on the same flight as well. After separation, however, the booster determined that the conditions required for a catch had not been met, and its course was diverted to the Gulf of Mexico. It came down in the water and exploded; engineers are investigating the cause. SpaceX had previously caught the booster with the tower three times. So the goal of bringing both parts of the rocket back intact on the same flight was not achieved this time.

## Why it matters

Bringing a rocket's upper stage back from orbit and reusing it has long been a problem the space industry could not solve. Even on Falcon 9, only the first stage comes back. Catching with the tower also saves weight by making landing legs unnecessary. The next test is to show that the same ship can be quickly refurbished and fly again.
