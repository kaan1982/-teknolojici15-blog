On Tuesday, 3 November, Google announced a new rule for its products used in schools. According to a blog post by Chris Phillips, the company's vice president for education, Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom and Google Search will come fully switched off on K-12 student accounts, from primary school through high school. Students will not be able to use them unless school administrators explicitly turn them on.

## The real debate is about Search

At first glance this looks like a concession to parents who are wary of AI. As CNN reported, Google had switched Gemini on by default in Classroom in August; the new decision reverses that. But there is a catch: Phillips told the New York Times that search and AI are "deeply intertwined" and that schools banning AI completely will lose access to Search as well. In that case, students will see a message saying their school has turned off search.

The backlash came quickly. New York mayor Zohran Mamdani, who restricted generative AI in classrooms this year, wrote that no company should dictate how children are educated. Google posted a statement on X the same day, saying schools can turn on Gemini, Gemini Notebook and Search separately, and arguing that this means "more choice, not less." Still, the statement implies that the AI summaries and AI Mode inside Search will stay in place.

Google also announced a "Guided Learning" feature: when a student pastes in a homework question, they will get step-by-step guidance instead of a direct answer. The feature is available in Classroom worldwide starting today; for Search, a pilot begins this month in some US schools. Details are to be shared with schools in the coming months.

## Why it matters

Thanks to Chromebooks, Google is infrastructure that tens of millions of students use every day. Once search and AI become inseparable, the "school without AI" option in practice means "school without Google." That weakens the hand of schools that want to keep AI out of the classroom.
