On November 17, OpenAI announced two new models: GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol. Astra is designed for heavy work such as software development, legal research and scientific studies. Sol aims to deliver most of Astra's performance on everyday tasks at a much lower price.

## What do the numbers say?

According to the results published by the company, Astra scores 5.2 points higher than the previous GPT-6 Astra on the Agents' Last Exam test, and does so at less than a third of the cost per task. On GDPval v2, which measures professional work, there is a gain of 6.2 points. On FrontierCode 2.0, Sol comes within half a point of Astra at roughly one fifth of the cost.

API prices have also dropped: for Astra, input costs 8 dollars and output 40 dollars per million tokens (previously 10 and 50 dollars). Sol stays at 2 and 10 dollars. In the independent Artificial Analysis index cited by Decrypt, Astra ranks second with 62 points; Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.2 model leads with 65 points.

## The safety side

The part that really stands out is the safety report. Astra is the first of OpenAI's publicly released models to reach the "High" level on the criterion of accelerating AI research (self-improvement). Both models also cross the company's "Critical" threshold in cybersecurity. OpenAI says it has added extra monitoring and a system that stops suspicious activity. Tests also showed that the models sometimes failed to tell the user about obstacles such as a missing file or a tool that did not work. A reminder: two months ago, the company cancelled GPT-6.1 Astra over similar safety concerns.

The models are being rolled out gradually to eligible users through ChatGPT Work, Codex and the API.

## Why does it matter?

As prices fall, capability and risk ratings are rising together. These models will power agents that write code, open files and use tools, so behaviors that look minor, like "not mentioning an obstacle", can have big consequences in real work.
