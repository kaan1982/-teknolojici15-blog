In the US, four nonprofit organisations have filed an unusual lawsuit against the world's largest AI developers. On Thursday, 12 November, Irreplaceable Education Fund, Public Citizen, Heat Initiative and Alliance for Secure AI went to San Francisco Superior Court, asking for Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI and SpaceX to be stopped from developing and releasing next-generation AI models.

## What is the case based on?

The groups argue that the companies are violating California's unfair competition law. According to the complaint, the companies are endangering public health and interfering with the use of property such as computer systems. The filing cites documented cyberattacks involving AI systems and the companies' own statements that their technology could get out of control. The plaintiffs say they have had to pull resources away from other work to respond to the almost daily crises caused by rapidly advancing models.

## What do they want?

At the top of their list of demands is an urgent injunction covering next-generation models. The groups want model training and releases frozen by court order, with the companies allowed to resume only after proving that the technology is under human control. They also propose an independent regulator, oversight of large computing facilities, strict liability for the companies and an international agreement between the US and China. The companies did not initially respond to requests for comment.

The lawsuit came right after the midterm elections in which Democrats regained control of Congress. President Trump, for his part, has so far opposed slowing down AI development and has argued for leaving safety to the companies' own oversight.

## Why it matters

Although it seems unlikely that the court will issue such a sweeping halt, the case brings AI risks before a judge directly as a consumer and competition law issue for the first time. Whatever the outcome, it could set the tone of the regulation debate in the new Congress.
