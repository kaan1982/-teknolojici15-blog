The board of Nvidia, one of the world's most valuable chip companies, announced on Sunday, 8 November, that it had launched an independent review concerning CEO Jensen Huang. The review was prompted by recent reports claiming that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort.

## What did the board say?

According to the written statement, the review will be conducted by a committee made up solely of independent board members, supported by outside independent legal counsel. The board stressed that it considers the national and economic security of the United States a priority. It said it has so far seen no reason to believe that national security or Nvidia's secrets have been compromised, but that it will examine all available information. Huang was said to fully support the review and to be cooperating; the board added that it has confidence in Huang and the management team. No timeline was given for the review.

## Background

According to reports by Bloomberg and Axios, US intelligence officials suspect that an executive at Megaspeed, a company that spends billions of dollars on Nvidia chips, works for Chinese intelligence; Huang reportedly had a close relationship with this person. These allegations are not yet based on any official finding. In a letter to employees, Huang acknowledged a mistake in his private life and said he takes full responsibility.

On 5 November, three committee chairs in the US House of Representatives asked Huang to brief them in a closed-door session on 18 November. Nvidia shares fell 9.26 percent on Thursday after the reports and closed Friday at $186.64, about 22 percent below their record level. The company will report quarterly results on 17 November.

## Why it matters

Nvidia's chips sit at the foundation of AI infrastructure, and their export is at the center of the technology rivalry between the US and China. A security review of the company's best-known executive matters closely to both investors and chip export policy.
