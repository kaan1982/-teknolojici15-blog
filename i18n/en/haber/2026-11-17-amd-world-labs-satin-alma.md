Chipmaker AMD announced on November 16 that it had completed its acquisition of the AI lab World Labs. The deal, first announced on September 28, was paid entirely in stock and is worth about $8.2 billion. AMD's own announcement gives no price; the figure is based on the value on the day the deal was first announced.

## What does World Labs do?

Founded in 2024, World Labs develops "world models" that generate and animate interactive 3D environments. The company's newest model, Atlas, came out on November 12 and can build and reconstruct 3D scenes from photos or generated images. Its first product, Marble, produces 3D worlds that can be edited and downloaded. The target audience includes film and visual effects teams, architecture firms and researchers who train their robots in virtual environments.

## Who goes where?

World Labs founder Fei-Fei Li, one of the best-known names in computer vision, has become executive vice president and chief scientist at AMD. Li will report directly to AMD CEO Lisa Su. Together with Justin Johnson and Ben Mildenhall, she will continue to lead the World Labs team inside AMD. According to a company filing cited by VP Land, AMD issued 14,978,322 shares to former World Labs shareholders.

The relationship between the two companies is not new: in 2025 they launched a technical partnership to improve model training and inference on AMD GPUs. Lisa Su says computing platforms and models must be developed together for the next era of AI infrastructure, and that the team's research will shape AMD's future hardware, software and systems.

## Why does it matter?

Instead of just selling hardware, chipmakers are now also bringing in the teams that build the models running on that hardware. With this move, AMD both steps into a new field such as robotics simulation and gains the chance to design its chips around the newest kinds of models.
