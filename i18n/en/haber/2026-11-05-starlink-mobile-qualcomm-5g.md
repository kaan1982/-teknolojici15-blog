SpaceX has taken a concrete step in its plan to become a mobile operator in the US. Under a multi-year deal Qualcomm announced on 4 November, the radio and signal processing technology for Starlink Mobile's terrestrial 5G network will come from Qualcomm. According to The Next Web, the deal covers tens of thousands of sites across the US, and the rollout will begin in 2027. Financial terms were not disclosed.

## What hardware?

As RCR Wireless reported, every site will use Qualcomm's Dragonwing infrastructure platforms. The QRU100 chip will sit inside the radio units mounted on masts, while the QDU100 will handle signal processing behind those radios. The radios will have a "massive MIMO" design with 64 transmit and 64 receive antenna paths, so a single station can steer separate signals to many phones at once. Qualcomm says the solution delivers higher performance per watt.

## Satellite and tower together

Until now, SpaceX's mobile work had been limited to services such as sending messages from satellites to ordinary phones. Starlink's Michael Nicolls said the goal is to combine satellites and terrestrial cell towers in a single network. According to The Next Web, SpaceX expanded its spectrum holdings with frequencies it bought from EchoStar in 2025 and will build the network in stages across different frequency bands.

Analysts are still cautious. An expert speaking to RCR Wireless stressed that SpaceX's 5G spectrum is still limited and that advanced antennas cannot make up for that shortfall on their own. The company reportedly aims to become the fourth largest mobile operator in the US.

## Why it matters

An operator that designs its satellite and terrestrial networks together from the start promises seamless connectivity with the same phone in the countryside and the city. That means a new and ambitious rival in a US mobile market that three big companies have shared for years.
