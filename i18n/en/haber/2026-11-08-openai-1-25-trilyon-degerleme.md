OpenAI closed the first part of its new funding round on 6 November. According to reports from Bloomberg and Reuters, about $11 billion landed in the company's accounts, and the round values OpenAI at $1.25 trillion. The company's chief financial officer, Sarah Friar, confirmed the valuation in a television interview.

## Below target

The press had earlier reported that OpenAI wanted to raise $30 billion or more. For the valuation first targeted, sources give figures between $1.4 trillion and $1.5 trillion. Friar said the $1.5 trillion figure came from a media report, not from the company. That means the $11 billion raised amounts to a little more than a third of the original target.

The round is not over yet. According to Bloomberg, remaining commitments could bring the total to as much as $20 billion in the coming weeks. Participating investors include SoftBank and Abu Dhabi-based MGX; the round's lead investor and terms were not disclosed.

## Why the cooling now?

Over the past month, expectations for AI companies have cooled. Growing voter backlash against data centers after the US midterm elections, along with possible regulation, has unsettled tech investors. According to Reuters, the valuation being discussed for Anthropic, which is preparing to go public on the Nasdaq this month, has also fallen from about $2 trillion to around $1.5 trillion.

Friar, however, argues that the business side is strong: she said the company grew 70 percent in the third quarter compared with the previous quarter and that ChatGPT has reached 1.2 billion weekly users, but she gave no revenue figure.

## Why it matters

OpenAI is still one of the world's most valuable startups, but this round shows that investors are no longer printing unlimited money for AI. For companies tied to OpenAI's computing commitments, such as Nvidia, how the round closes is also an important signal.
