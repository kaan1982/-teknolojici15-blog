US chip company Marvell Technology has reached an agreement to acquire Lumentum, a maker of lasers and optical components. In the all-stock transaction announced on November 2, Lumentum's enterprise value is put at about $115 billion. That makes it one of the largest acquisitions in technology history.

## The terms of the deal

Each Lumentum share will be converted into 4.5 Marvell shares. According to the companies, that works out to about $1,132 per share and a 23.9 percent premium to the October 28 closing price. When the deal is completed, Marvell shareholders will own about 67 percent of the combined company and Lumentum shareholders 33 percent. Three executives, including Lumentum CEO Michael Hurlston, will join Marvell's board of directors.

Closing is expected in the first half of 2028; it requires approval from both companies' shareholders and from regulators. According to the filing submitted to the SEC, Marvell may have to pay Lumentum a termination fee of up to $5.8 billion if the deal falls apart under certain circumstances.

## Why light?

In AI data centers, the data flowing between thousands of chips is pushing against the limit of what copper cables can carry. Optical links that carry data with light are faster and use less energy. Marvell designs connectivity chips and custom silicon, while Lumentum makes lasers and optical transceivers. The companies expect the combined business to deliver more than $1 billion in annual cost savings. Marvell CEO Matt Murphy described the opportunity as "once in a generation".

The stock market was cautious on day one, though: according to Investing.com data, both stocks fell by double digits on November 2.

## Why does it matter?

The chip race is no longer won only by the speed of processors, but also by the speed of the links that connect them. This acquisition shows that optical connectivity has moved to the center of AI infrastructure.
